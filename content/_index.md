I got started in game programming—C++ and Unity, mostly. Games are unforgiving that way: the thing either runs smoothly or it very obviously doesn't, and you can't really talk your way around a broken frame rate. That habit of caring whether something actually works stuck with me, and it's what I bring to everything else I build.

I use AI-assisted tools to move quickly, but speed isn't really the point—it's knowing what the fast version skips and fixing it before anyone else has to deal with it. On the projects here, that meant noticing a leaderboard that trusted the browser a little too much, a login that looked secure but wasn't really, and an AI feature that would have fallen apart the moment someone pasted in something weird. Small things, but they're the difference between a demo and something you'd actually ship.

I like getting to a working version fast, then slowing down for the parts that matter.
